# SceneRight

**The photo you meant to take.**  
Frames for you. Shows you why.

Identity direction 03 · October 2026

## The name

**SceneRight** connects the scene in front of you with the picture you intended to take. Spoken aloud, it also evokes “seen right”: the satisfaction of a photograph that matches the moment.

Write the product name **SceneRight**, with capital S and R. The logo uses a lowercase **sceneright** wordmark; **photo** is a small visual descriptor, not part of the product name. Say the name as “scene right.” Do not split it into two words in written product references.

The user approved SceneRight on October 19, 2026. Preliminary exact-name web and store-index searches did not surface an obvious camera-app match. Domain availability, direct storefront checks, similar marks, and trademark rights remain unconfirmed. This approval establishes the creative direction; it is not legal clearance. No registration has been made.

## The promise

For people who love photos but never learned photography, SceneRight is a calm camera designed to bring the picture closer to the moment they saw. One quiet cue, a well-timed shot, and a little understanding afterward.

The emotional outcome is recognition: “That’s how it looked.” The practical outcome is more keepers. The teaching happens in the user's picture, without interrupting the reason they raised their phone.

### Story in one breath — intended product, pending release evidence

You raise your phone and the photo never looks like the moment did. SceneRight shows you where to move with one quiet cue, takes the shot when everything lines up, then explains in your own picture what worked and one thing to try next time. It learns what you keep. Nothing leaves your phone.

This paragraph describes the brief, not an implemented or verified product. Use the landing page's development language until the app's release behavior has been checked.

**A familiar camera, with a warmer welcome.** Warm charcoal anchors the system. Ivory makes the surrounding space feel personal. A small apricot catchlight adds humanity. Photographs carry the richness and variety.

Use approximately 75% neutral surface, 20% Ink, and 5% Apricot in brand layouts. This is a compositional guide, not a fixed UI requirement. Keep live-camera surfaces dark and neutral so photographs remain easy to judge. Keep the existing green ready and amber warning signals; the precise existing color values were not supplied and are not redefined in this package. Apricot belongs to the identity and must not replace functional status colors. Use opaque neutral chips for cue text. Do not wash photographs in brand colors.

Use Ink text on Apricot. Apricot is not a light-surface text color. Use Paper text on Ink. Color must never be the only way to signal alignment, capture readiness, selection, or errors.

### Typography

The outlined wordmark uses DejaVu Sans with adjusted spacing. Its typography is already converted to vector paths; it does not require a font installation. Preserve its supplied letter spacing and proportions.

Use DejaVu Sans for the site, controls, cues, and explanations. Use DejaVu Serif for large brand headlines only. Android may use the platform sans for native controls. Never use the editorial serif for a live cue. Bundled webfonts and their license are in `brand/site/assets/`.

Body copy should normally be at least 16 sp in the app. Live cues should normally be 18–22 sp, with enough room for larger system text. Interactive targets should be at least 48 dp. Do not shrink essential cue text to preserve a layout.

### Mark

A circular eye/lens ring surrounds a pupil with one offset catchlight. The ring suggests looking and framing. The glint adds the small moment of recognition. The mark is intentionally simple enough to work without lettering.

- Clear space: at least one-quarter of the mark's outer diameter on all sides.
- Minimum standalone mark: 24 px. At 16 px, use only as a favicon with optical review.
- Minimum lockup width: 240 px with the descriptor. Below that, use the standalone wordmark or mark.
- Never stretch, rotate, add a shadow to the mark itself, or make the pupil a recording indicator.
- Use primary on Paper; reverse on Ink; true single-ink artwork for monochrome contexts.
- Keep the catchlight at the supplied size and position. Do not animate it to imply monitoring.

The rounded icon masks in `identity/previews/` are previews. The Play icon export is a square without baked-in rounding.

## Voice

A calm friend with a good eye who speaks only when it helps. Physical, specific, brief, and optional when possible. Address the picture, not the user's ability.

### Live cues

One cue at a time, one to three physical words. “Slightly right.” “Level.” “Step closer.” “Hold still.” Do not stack directions or use the cue surface for praise, instruction history, scoring, or an explanation.

### After the shot

Describe one visible thing that worked in this photograph. Offer one suggestion for next time. Let the user dismiss the explanation without losing the picture.

Example observation: “The space around the tree keeps it clear.”  
Example suggestion: “Try next time: move a little closer.”

Ground the observation in actual visual evidence. Do not invent a subject, lighting condition, or result. Keep the observation and suggestion separate so Maya can leave and Tom can keep reading.

### Language rules

The app never says “I”, uses exclamation marks, or judges the person taking the picture. Avoid absolute praise or failure language. Describe a change the person can make.

Banned marketing and product words: AI, smart, pro, rule of thirds, perfect, wrong, streak, badge, cloud, account, revolutionary. These words appear here only to document the restriction. Prefer “keeper”, “on your phone”, and “try next time”.

## Claims follow the release

All three supplied product truths remain **unverified** in this branding task. No camera app source was supplied or built. The landing page therefore describes them as plans.

| Intended truth | Evidence to require before changing copy to present tense |
| --- | --- |
| It stays on your phone | Check the final merged release manifest for absence of network permission; audit dependencies and all data paths; explicitly verify backup, device transfer, logging, and any sharing behavior. | It shoots at the best moment | Verify automatic capture with representative scenes and define what conditions trigger it. Prefer “takes the shot when everything lines up” over an unqualified promise of the absolute best moment. |
| It learns your taste | Verify that keep choices affect later behavior, that the learned preferences stay local, and that the user can reset them. |

If only part ships, describe that part. Do not use a development mockup as evidence, add fabricated reviews, or imply store availability. The website has no invented download link, launch date, pricing, or mailing list.

## Photography and motion

Show ordinary moments with something worth noticing: window light, an unposed expression, a familiar place. Keep a natural range of skin tones and subjects. Choose pictures that feel achievable on a phone. Show framing help before filters or dramatic retouching.

The included meadow is original vector illustration for a labeled concept preview. It is not a photo taken by the app. Replace it with verified app captures before release marketing.

Use short transitions around 180 ms when needed. Respect reduced motion. Do not use continuous pulsing, spinning, confetti, or progress mechanics in the camera experience.
