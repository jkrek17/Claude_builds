# Naming: Composition Coach becomes Eyebright

**Recommendation: Eyebright.** Backup: **Quiet Eye**.
**Tagline:** *The photo you meant to take.*
**One sentence:** Eyebright is a camera that frames the shot for you, takes it at the best moment, and
then shows you, in your own photo, what worked and one thing to try next time, all on your phone.

The rest of this file is the working: the brief, 34 candidates scored, the collision checks, the
shortlist and the argument.

## The brief, in one line

For people who love photos but never learned photography: the camera frames for you, coaches you
afterwards from your own photo, and learns what you like. On-device, nothing leaves the phone.

What the name has to do: promise a better eye without sounding like a lesson, a filter, or a pro tool.
It has to sit under an icon (10 characters or fewer), survive being said out loud once, and feel like a
camera, not a social app.

## Shortlist

1. **Eyebright**: a wildflower once used to "clear the eye". Says *you will see better*, owns its
   word in both stores, and turns into an icon by itself (a bright eye, a catchlight).
2. **Quiet Eye**: the calm, steady look that comes just before a precise action. The most exact
   description of one quiet cue and a well-timed shutter. One syllable too long.
3. **Good Eye**: the compliment every person who takes photos wants to hear. Best promise on the
   list; blocked by a same-name camera-snap app in pre-launch.
4. **Well Seen**: an old compliment, warm and modest. Loses points when heard ("Well Scene").
5. **Kept**: the verdict as a name, one syllable, four letters. Too close to Google Keep on the very
   platform we ship on, and an iOS photo journal already uses it.

## The recommendation: Eyebright

**It says the promise without a lesson in it.** People who love photos but never learned photography
do not want a coach, a pro, or a rule. They want to see better. "Eyebright" says that in two everyday
words fused into one: your eye, made bright. It is the outcome, not the method.

**It ties the whole product together.** The app already has a screen called "Your eye". The verdict
is "Keeper". The catchlight, the small bright point that makes an eye look alive in a portrait, is
the icon. The name, the mark and the most personal screen all say the same thing.

**It has a story worth one sentence.** Eyebright is a small meadow flower (*Euphrasia*) that, for
centuries, people used to clear their sight. We do not lean on the herbal side, but the story is
true, gentle and easy to tell in an About screen.

**It passes the practical tests.** Two syllables. Spelled the way it sounds. Nine characters, so
it sits under a launcher icon without truncation. No camera or photo app uses it in either store;
the .app and .camera domains show no sign of use.

**It feels like a camera, not a social app.** No playful misspelling, no "-ly", no "snap". It sits
next to Halide, Obscura and Moment as a calm noun, not a slogan.

**What it costs.** "Eyebright" competes in web search with herbal eye drops, and Play's "eye" search
results are full of eye-exercise apps. The store title carries a descriptor for that reason
(*Eyebright: Camera Coach*), and the short description leads with "camera".

**Backup: Quiet Eye.** If trademark clearance fails on Eyebright, Quiet Eye is clear in both stores,
has free domains, and describes the live experience better than any other name here. It breaks the
two-syllable rule by one, and the tagline and identity carry over unchanged (the ring-and-catchlight
mark is an eye either way).

## Tagline and description

- **Tagline (5 words):** The photo you meant to take.
- **Support line (used under the tagline):** Frames for you. Shows you why.
- **One-sentence description:** Eyebright is a camera that frames the shot for you, takes it at the
  best moment, and then shows you, in your own photo, what worked and one thing to try next time, all
  on your phone.

Tagline alternatives considered: "Frames for you. Shows you why." (6, kept as support line), "See it.
Keep it." (4, too generic), "Your eye, only brighter." (4, cute, leans on the name), "Point. Tap.
Keeper." (3, sounds like an instruction card).

## Decision (19 October 2026)

After the Eyebright recommendation above, the founder reviewed a "justSo photo" identity board.
**Just So** was gated: *Just So Camera* is already on Google Play.

Alternatives screened afterwards, with results:

| Name | Result |
|---|---|
| everSo, likeSo, evenSo, ohSo | No store apps found under these names. evenSo sits near "Even Longer", an iOS long-exposure camera app. |
| SecondEye | Only a Gemini developer-competition project uses it; no store app found. |
| TrainedEye | Clear: nothing found in either store. |
| ReFrame | Gated: "ReFrame: Multi-Angle Recorder" (iOS camera app, 2026) and "Reframe AI" (photo editor). |
| Nudge | No camera app found. NudgeCam was a 2010 research project; the word is generic. |
| JustRight | Chosen first and shipped for one build. No camera or photo app found under "JustRight" or "Just Right" in either store; nearest names JustSnap and Just Cam. Replaced the same day. |
| SeenRight | No app found. Nearest: "Seen Camera" (iOS selfie-video interview tool) and Seene (2013 3D-photo app). Passed over for its folksy grammar. |
| **SceneRight** | **Chosen.** No app found under "SceneRight" or "Scene Right" in either store. Nearest names: ColorRight (2010), Scene Scout, "Scene: Organize & Share Photos". |

**SceneRight** is the name. Conditions that come with it:

- "scene" is a generic word, so the store title carries a descriptor: **SceneRight: Camera Coach**.
- Trademark clearance is still required before launch.
- Eyebright, the earlier recommendation, and JustRight, the first choice, are retired; the tagline
  and support line carry over unchanged. The identity is rebuilt for the new name (`docs/BRAND.md`, `brand/identity/`).
